Understand how Transmission Control Protocol manages reliable communication and controls network congestion.

01. What is TCP?

Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) is a connection-oriented transport layer protocol that provides reliable data transmission between devices over a network.

It ensures that data reaches the destination in the correct order, detects transmission errors and retransmits lost data when necessary.

02. What is Congestion Control?

TCP Congestion Control is a mechanism used to regulate the amount of data transmitted into a network.

When too much data enters a network, congestion can occur, causing packet loss and increased delay. TCP adjusts its transmission rate to reduce congestion and maintain efficient communication.

05. Four Phases of TCP Congestion Control

01 — Slow Start

In Slow Start, the congestion window grows exponentially as acknowledgements are received. This continues until the congestion window reaches the slow start threshold or congestion is detected.

02 — Congestion Avoidance

In Congestion Avoidance, the congestion window increases approximately linearly. This provides a more controlled increase in the transmission rate.

03 — Fast Retransmit

When the sender receives three duplicate ACKs, it assumes that a segment may have been lost and retransmits the missing segment without waiting for a timeout.

04 — Fast Recovery

In TCP Reno, Fast Recovery adjusts the congestion window after three duplicate ACKs and allows transmission to continue without restarting the entire congestion control process.
