TCP FUNDAMENTALS

Understanding TCP & Congestion Control

Explore the fundamentals of TCP, why congestion occurs, and how TCP manages network traffic efficiently.

01 · INTRODUCTION

What is TCP?

Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) is a connection-oriented transport layer protocol that provides reliable and ordered data delivery between devices over a network.

It uses acknowledgements, sequence numbers and retransmissions to ensure that data reaches its destination correctly.

02 · BASIC CONCEPT

What is Congestion?

Network congestion occurs when the amount of data entering a network exceeds its available capacity, causing delays, packet loss and increased traffic.

03 · IMPORTANCE

Why is Congestion Control Needed?

It prevents network overload, reduces packet loss, controls transmission rates and improves overall network performance and stability.

Observation guide

  1. During Slow Start, the congestion window grows exponentially (approximately doubles per RTT in this model).
  2. After reaching ssthresh, the simulation enters Congestion Avoidance and increases cwnd linearly.
  3. Three duplicate ACKs trigger Fast Retransmit and Fast Recovery in the Reno-style model.
  4. A retransmission timeout reduces cwnd to 1 MSS and restarts Slow Start.
  5. Packet loss reduces the sending window, helping TCP respond to possible congestion.
