KNOW THE CONCEPTS

TCP congestion control

TCP congestion control regulates how much data a sender injects into the network to reduce the risk of overwhelming network resources.

CORE IDEA

What is congestion?

Network congestion occurs when the amount of data entering the network exceeds the available capacity. Queues may grow, delay may increase and packets may be dropped.

TCP uses feedback, including acknowledgements and loss signals, to adjust its sending rate.

VARIABLE

cwnd

Congestion window: limits the amount of unacknowledged data a sender may have in the network.

VARIABLE

ssthresh

Slow-start threshold: marks the transition from Slow Start to Congestion Avoidance in this model.

PHASE 01

Slow Start

Starts with a small congestion window. In the simplified model, cwnd approximately doubles each round-trip time (RTT) while below ssthresh.

PHASE 02

Congestion Avoidance

Once the threshold is reached, cwnd grows more cautiously, approximately one maximum segment size (MSS) per RTT.

PHASE 03

Fast Retransmit

Three duplicate ACKs are treated as a sign that a segment may have been lost. TCP retransmits the missing segment without waiting for a timeout.

PHASE 04

Fast Recovery

TCP Reno reduces its sending window after duplicate ACKs and then continues with Congestion Avoidance after recovery.

TIMEOUT EVENT

Retransmission timeout

A timeout is treated as a stronger congestion signal. In this teaching model, ssthresh is reduced and cwnd returns to 1 MSS before Slow Start begins again.

Model note: This website uses a simplified, educational TCP Reno-style model measured in MSS units and RTT rounds. Real TCP implementations include additional rules and implementation-specific details.

Observation guide

  1. During Slow Start, the congestion window grows exponentially (approximately doubles per RTT in this model).
  2. After reaching ssthresh, the simulation enters Congestion Avoidance and increases cwnd linearly.
  3. Three duplicate ACKs trigger Fast Retransmit and Fast Recovery in the Reno-style model.
  4. A retransmission timeout reduces cwnd to 1 MSS and restarts Slow Start.
  5. Packet loss reduces the sending window, helping TCP respond to possible congestion.
